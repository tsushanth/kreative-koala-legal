Because InboxIQ requests SMS, MMS, Contacts, notification and microphone permissions, this section describes exactly how that data is handled, in addition to the general terms above. It covers the 0.4.x versions and later versions, which differ only in the optional "Connect to Claude" feature described below.

  • Message content, phone numbers, and contact names are read and stored in a local database on your device. KreativeKoalaSolutions LLC does not receive them, sell them, or use them for advertising, profiling, or training AI models.
  • Sorting runs entirely on your device using a machine-learning model bundled with the app. Message text is not sent to a cloud AI service or any external server to sort it. An optional larger on-device model can be downloaded through Google Play; that download contains no data from your phone.
  • Nothing leaves your phone through InboxIQ. Google Play versions released after 0.4.x do not request the internet permission, so the app has no way to send your messages anywhere. You can check this yourself: Android's app info lists InboxIQ's permissions (the internet permission is not among them), and sorting your messages works the same in airplane mode.
  • Why each permission is used.
    • SMS and MMS (receive, send, read, receive MMS, receive WAP push): required to be your default SMS app. InboxIQ uses them to receive your texts and picture messages, to send the texts you write, and to read the messages already on your phone so it can copy them into its own database.
    • Contacts: to show a saved contact's name instead of a raw phone number, and, if you turn on auto-reply, to recognise people you have saved. Read locally only; contacts are not copied anywhere.
    • Notifications: to alert you to new messages, with Reply, Mark as read and Copy code buttons.
    • Microphone: only while you hold the voice-memo button. The audio is turned into text by a model on your device, is kept in memory only for that purpose, and is not saved or sent.
  • Android's shared message storage. While InboxIQ is your default SMS app it also writes the texts you receive and send to Android's shared SMS/MMS storage, as every default SMS app must, so the app you switch back to still has them. Deleting a message or conversation in InboxIQ removes it from InboxIQ only; the copy in Android's shared storage stays until you delete it there or in another SMS app.
  • Auto-reply (optional, off by default, in versions that include it). If you turn it on, InboxIQ sends one short text you have approved to people who message you while it is on, only to people you have texted before or saved as contacts, never to businesses, short codes, groups, code or spam senders, at most once per person per day and 20 a day. These are ordinary text messages sent through your carrier, which may charge for them; nothing about them is sent to us.
  • Backup and restore (in versions that include it). You can save your conversations (message text, numbers, labels, pins, archive and mute settings, and blocked numbers; not photos) to a file you choose with Android's file picker, and restore from such a file. InboxIQ does not upload the file and does not encrypt it. Where you store it, and that service's privacy practices, are up to you. Restored messages are added to InboxIQ only, not to Android's shared storage.
  • Rating prompt. InboxIQ may occasionally ask you to rate it using Google Play's in-app review prompt. It decides when using counters stored only on your device (app opens and replies sent); no message content is involved, and the prompt itself is provided by Google Play under Google's privacy policy.
  • InboxIQ has no accounts, no advertising SDK, and no analytics.
  • Optional "Connect to Claude" (earlier 0.4.x versions and any build that includes it; off by default). Google Play versions of InboxIQ released after 0.4.x do not include this feature and have no internet permission, so they cannot send your messages anywhere. In versions that include it, nothing leaves your phone unless you turn it on and accept an on-screen disclosure. While InboxIQ is open on your screen, it then lets Claude, signed in to your own Claude account, read the message text, sender numbers, contact names, and timestamps that Claude asks for, and send replies you approve. This is relayed through a service run by KreativeKoalaSolutions LLC, which holds only your verified phone number and access tokens and does not store or log message content. Linking requires a verification code delivered by a voice call to your number. Turning the feature off or tapping Disconnect revokes access, and you can also remove the connector in your Claude settings.
  • Uninstalling InboxIQ deletes its locally stored data along with the app; files you saved with Backup stay where you put them. To delete the relay link (phone number and tokens) for earlier versions, use Disconnect in the app or email puzzleverseai@gmail.com.

11. App-Specific Disclosures: VoxKey

VoxKey is a voice-dictation app for Android. You tap a text box in another app, tap the VoxKey bubble, and speak, and VoxKey types what you said. It can also be used as a keyboard. VoxKey works in two modes, and what happens to your voice is different in each. Because VoxKey requests Microphone, Internet and Accessibility access, this section describes exactly how that data is handled, in addition to the general terms above.

Mode 1: free mode, using your phone's speech recognizer (the default)

  • Microphone audio is used only to turn your speech into text as you dictate. In free mode, VoxKey asks the speech recognizer built into your phone (Android's SpeechRecognizer service, provided by your device manufacturer or Google) to listen and return the text. VoxKey uses an on-device recognizer where your phone has one and asks for offline recognition, but Android's documentation says this service is likely to stream audio to remote servers and that the offline preference may have no effect (see Android's SpeechRecognizer documentation). Whether your audio is processed on your phone or sent to the provider's servers therefore depends on your phone, the recognizer it uses and your own speech and Google settings, and that provider's privacy policy and settings apply, not this one. VoxKey does not receive that audio, only the resulting text. If you download VoxKey's offline speech model and turn it on, VoxKey records the microphone itself while you dictate and recognizes your speech inside VoxKey, on your phone; that audio is kept only in memory until the text is ready and is never stored or sent.
  • Local AI cleanup (punctuation and capitalization) is optional and runs entirely on-device using a language model downloaded to your device. Your dictated text is not sent anywhere for this processing.
  • Model downloads: to enable offline speech or local AI cleanup, VoxKey downloads model files from our infrastructure (hosted by Cloudflare) to your device, only when you ask it to. This is a one-directional file download; no dictated text, audio, or personal data is uploaded as part of it.

Mode 2: VoxKey Cloud (optional, off by default)

VoxKey Cloud is an optional paid plan, with a free 10-minute trial. Nothing is sent to VoxKey Cloud unless you turn on "Use cloud dictation" and agree on the consent screen shown before the first cloud dictation. You can turn it off at any time with the same switch, and VoxKey then goes back to Mode 1.

  • What is sent while Cloud dictation is on: the audio you dictate (streamed while you speak), the words in your VoxKey dictionary, your dictation language and your selected writing style. After speech recognition, the recognized text is sent on for text cleanup (punctuation, capitalization, removing filler words). VoxKey does not send the text already in the box you are typing into, or other screen content.
  • Who processes it: audio and text go over an encrypted connection to a server we run on Cloudflare, and from there to speech-recognition and text-cleanup providers that process them for us. Today these are Soniox (speech recognition), with ElevenLabs as a backup; and, for text cleanup, Cloudflare Workers AI, with Fireworks AI and Google (Gemini) as backups. As a last resort for cleanup or backup recognition the service can also use Groq or OpenAI. These providers handle your audio and text under their own terms and privacy policies. We do not sell your audio or text and do not use it for advertising.
  • What we keep: our server does not write your audio or the text to storage; audio is held in memory only while a dictation is processed and then discarded. Our server logs hold technical records only: a shortened one-way hash of an internal user identifier, which speech provider was used, the number of seconds, timings, a cost figure and a success or failure status. They contain no audio, no text and no dictionary words. Cloudflare, which hosts the server, keeps these logs for a limited time (up to 7 days) and, as with any web request, also handles standard connection data such as your IP address in serving the request. We also keep a running count of the seconds each subscription or free trial has used this month and today, tied to the same one-way hash, so that we can enforce the 900-minute monthly limit.
  • What the providers may keep: we cannot promise that nothing is retained by the providers. Some may keep audio or text for a limited time for security and abuse prevention, as described in their own terms. We do not use your Cloud audio to train our own models. We have not independently audited the providers' practices, and whether a provider may use content to improve its own services depends on that provider's terms and on the type of account we hold with it.
  • Identifiers and purchase checks: to confirm your subscription, VoxKey sends the Google Play purchase token to our server, which checks it with Google Play. The purchase is tagged with a random identifier that VoxKey generates on your phone (not your name, email, advertising ID or Android ID). Your payment details stay with Google Play; we never receive them. For the free trial, VoxKey also sends that random install code and a Google Play Integrity check, which lets Google confirm to us that VoxKey was installed from Google Play on a genuine device. To limit abuse, the free trial also uses a one-way hash of your IP address, kept only for the current day and never in clear text.
  • Cloud limits and fallbacks: Cloud dictation has a fair-use limit of 900 minutes per month and a per-dictation length limit. If Cloud is unavailable or you reach a limit, VoxKey falls back to Mode 1.

Permissions and your choices

  • Accessibility service: VoxKey's floating bubble uses Android's Accessibility service for these things only: to notice when you tap a text box, so the bubble can appear; to type your dictated text into that box; and to use the microphone while you dictate: in free mode by asking your phone's speech recognizer to listen, and, if you use offline speech or VoxKey Cloud, by recording the microphone itself. To place the text at your cursor, VoxKey briefly reads the text already in that box. It does not store or transmit that text or other screen content, and the bubble never appears in password fields. You turn this permission on yourself and can turn it off at any time in Android's Accessibility settings.
  • Optional: Help improve VoxKey. You choose whether to share your dictated text, during setup or later in Settings. This is off unless you choose it, and you can change it at any time in Settings. If you opt in, VoxKey sends us (a) the text the speech recognizer heard and the text after VoxKey's cleanup step, (b) corrections you make to a dictation inside VoxKey's own editor screen, (c) a random install identifier that is not linked to your name, account or device identifiers, and (d) the app version and your selected language. We use this to develop, evaluate and improve VoxKey's speech and text-cleanup models. It can include anything you say. This sharing never includes audio, and nothing is collected from password fields or fields that ask keyboards not to learn. Text produced by your personal snippets, and your dictionary and snippet lists, are never sent. Shared text is stored with our infrastructure provider, Cloudflare, Inc., which processes it on our behalf, and is encrypted in transit. It is deleted automatically after 180 days.
  • Deleting what you shared: tap "Delete my shared data" in VoxKey's Settings to delete everything linked to your install identifier and stop sharing. Because shared data is linked only to that random identifier, if you uninstall VoxKey or clear its data before deleting, we cannot locate your data; it is still removed automatically after 180 days.
  • VoxKey Cloud data: we do not store your Cloud audio or text, so there is nothing of that kind for us to delete. To stop Cloud, turn off "Use cloud dictation" and cancel your subscription in Google Play (Manage subscription). The usage counts and logs we hold contain no audio or text and are linked only to a one-way hash, not to your name or email. If you have a privacy question or request, email support@kreativekoala.llc. Purchase records are also held by Google Play under Google's policies.
  • VoxKey does not include an advertising SDK, does not sell your data, and does not use your dictated content for advertising or profiling. VoxKey does not currently collect usage analytics. Other than VoxKey Cloud and the optional sharing described above, VoxKey does not transmit dictated text to us or to any analytics service.
  • Uninstalling VoxKey deletes the locally downloaded models and all locally stored data (dictation history, dictionary, snippets, settings) along with the app. It does not delete text you previously shared; use "Delete my shared data" first.

12. App-Specific Disclosures: NotifyPal

NotifyPal (Android) watches web pages you choose and tells you when they change. It has two parts that handle data differently.

Page watchers

  • What we collect: for each page you watch, the page address (URL), the instruction you type (for example "Track the price"), the check interval, the values we read from the page, and a random identifier the app generates on your device when you first open it. There is no account, and we do not collect your name, email address, phone number, or advertising ID.
  • Why: to check the page on a schedule from our server and to show you what changed. Your device asks our server for new changes about every 15 minutes and shows them as notifications on your device.
  • Service providers: our server runs on Fly.io and stores watcher data with Supabase (hosted on Amazon Web Services in the United States). When a new watcher is first checked (shortly after you add it), and again whenever the page layout changes so that the saved selectors no longer find anything, the page address (URL), the text and structure of that public web page, and your instruction are sent to Anthropic, whose AI model works out which part of the page to read. We do not send your random identifier to Anthropic. Our server's technical logs, kept by Fly.io, can include the addresses of the pages being checked.
  • Public pages only: NotifyPal cannot sign in to websites, and our server loads each page without your login. Do not watch addresses that contain personal tokens or pages that show personal information.
  • Retention and deletion: data is kept for as long as the watcher exists; we do not delete watchers or their readings automatically, and uninstalling the app does not remove them from our server. Deleting a watcher in the app stops its checks and asks our server to delete the watcher and its saved readings. If your phone is offline at that moment the request can fail without a message, and the watcher then stays on our server and may reappear in the app, so check the list again when you are back online. Because the random identifier is not linked to you, we cannot look up your data by name or email; delete your watchers in the app before uninstalling if you want them removed.
  • Android backup: NotifyPal allows Android's automatic backup, so Android may copy its random identifier and settings to your Google account and restore them, with your watchers, on a new phone.

Notification history (optional)

  • If you turn on Notification Access in Android settings, NotifyPal reads the notifications other apps show on your device (app name, title, text and time) so you can search them. It is off until you enable it.
  • These notifications are stored in a local database on your device until you clear them; the app does not expire them on its own. NotifyPal never sends them to our servers, shares them, or uses them for advertising. You can clear them in NotifyPal Settings, turn off Notification Access in Android settings, or uninstall the app to delete them from your phone.
  • Backups of this history: in NotifyPal 6.2.1 and earlier, Android's automatic backup (to your Google account) and phone-to-phone transfer can include this local database, so a copy may exist in your Google backup and be restored on a new phone. Newer versions exclude the notification database from backup and transfer. Backups held by Google are governed by your Google account settings; clearing the history in NotifyPal does not delete an older backup copy.

NotifyPal does not include advertising or analytics SDKs and does not sell your data.

SurfSense has no accounts or sign-in: it does not ask for or collect your name or email address, and it does not use AI. Where this policy refers to registration or account information, that does not apply to SurfSense.

  • What we collect: a random device ID and a random device key that SurfSense creates on your device the first time you use it; the name your device reports (for example "Pixel 9 Pro" or "mac-arm64"), so you can recognize it on your other paired devices; the number of seconds spent in each category on each day; when each device registered and when it last synced; and which of your devices are paired. Some device names contain a person's name; you can rename your device in your device settings.

Browser pages such as a new-tab page can also show up as a hostname. Chrome does not run extensions in incognito windows unless you allow it for SurfSense in Chrome's extension settings; if you allow it, the extension works there too. It keeps running totals by hostname on your device until they are sent to our server for sorting (when the day changes, or when you update from the popup), then clears them; if sending fails, they stay on your device until the next attempt. Hostnames are sent to our server only to be sorted into categories and are not saved in our database; only the per-category daily totals are stored.

To find an app's category, our server may look up the app's public Google Play page, which reveals the package name to Google, as any visit to that public page would. We also count, in aggregate, how many installs have synced and kept syncing, to tell whether the product works. Anyone who has that code can pair with your device and then see its category totals, so share it only with people you trust. You can unlink a device at any time in the app or extension, and pairings that have not been used for 30 days are removed automatically.
  • Service providers: our server runs on Fly.io and stores data with Supabase (hosted on Amazon Web Services in the United States). Data is sent over HTTPS.
  • Retention and deletion: daily totals and device records are kept until you ask us to delete them. Uninstalling the app or extension deletes the data stored on your device. To have the data on our server deleted, email us at the address in the Contact Us section at the end of this policy with your device name and the approximate dates you used SurfSense. TaskMasterBrowser is an Android web browser with an AI agent built in. You describe a task, and the agent opens websites, reads them, clicks, types, and fills in forms while you watch. Because it can send what it sees to an AI service, handle saved personal details, and talk to websites on your behalf, this section describes exactly how data is handled, in addition to the general terms above.

  • We run no server for this app and collect nothing. There are no accounts, no advertising, and no analytics of our own. KreativeKoalaSolutions LLC does not receive your tasks, pages, saved details, or API key.

Check your version in Android's app settings.
    • Version 1.1.1 and earlier: the key is an Anthropic API key. When you run a task, the app sends your task text and screenshots of the web pages the agent is working on directly from your phone to Anthropic (api.anthropic.com) under Anthropic's terms. Those screenshots can contain anything shown on the page. When you tap Test, the app also checks the key with Anthropic.

When you run a task, the app sends the following directly from your phone to OpenRouter, which passes it on to the company that runs the AI model you chose in Settings (for example OpenAI or Anthropic), under their terms: your task text; the title and web address of the page the agent is working on (the site and the first part of the path, not the part after a question mark); the readable text of the page (visible text, link and button names); the numbered list of buttons, links and fields the agent can use, including any text already entered in a field; and the agent's own short notes about the pages it has already seen. It does not send screenshots and never sends the contents of a password field. This is a best-effort check that can miss a field, and it does not apply to text shown elsewhere on the page; other things typed into a form, such as a name or an email address, are sent. The information sent can include anything on the page, including a page you have signed in to, so avoid running Cloud mode on a page that shows something you do not want an AI provider to read. The app asks OpenRouter to route each request only to providers that state they do not collect or store user data and that offer zero data retention (OpenRouter notes that some such providers may keep prompts briefly to prevent abuse); if no provider meets this for the model you chose, the request fails and the app does not retry with a less private setting. We cannot verify what OpenRouter or a model company actually does with your data: they handle it under their own policies, and we cannot delete what they hold. When you tap Test, the app also checks the key with OpenRouter. If an older Anthropic key is still stored after you update, the app no longer uses or sends it, and Settings has a button to delete it.

The agent uses Google's Gemma model, a roughly 2.6 GB file you download once from Hugging Face (huggingface.co) when you tap Download; the download contains no data from your phone, though Hugging Face sees the request, like any download. After that, the model runs on your phone, so the pages it reads are not sent to an AI service. It needs a 64-bit ARM phone with 8 GB of RAM or more.
  • Websites see what a browser sends. Pages load from the sites you visit, and anything the agent types into a page, including saved details you choose to fill, goes to that site as if you had typed it. The browser's home page is google.com, which loads when you open the app. If a task does not name a website, the app opens a Google search containing your task text.
  • Saved info for autofill (optional, on-device mode only). Names, addresses, and card details you save are encrypted on your phone with Android Keystore, are excluded from Android backups and device transfers, and are never uploaded. The on-device model does not see the saved values: they are masked before the model reads a page, and the app types the real value itself. Card scanning uses your camera app, reads the photo on your phone with Google's ML Kit, and deletes the photo straight away. Only text you confirm is saved. The app never types passwords. If you tap Import from contacts, Android's contact picker gives the app the name, email address, phone number and address of the one contact you pick, which you can then save; the app has no other access to your contacts.
  • You keep the last tap. Before the agent presses a button that looks like a purchase, a booking, a send or post, a delete, an unsubscribe or an account change, or before it leaves the websites your task names, it stops and asks you, and shows where the click goes. In Cloud mode it also refuses such an action if your task did not ask for it. The check reads the page's own buttons, forms and links, so a hostile or unusual page can still mislead it, it is not a guarantee, and it works best in English. Watch what the agent does, and use a virtual or single-use card number for purchases.
  • Third-party libraries. Card scanning uses Google ML Kit, which may send anonymous usage and performance diagnostics to Google. The app also uses a small library of ours that counts app opens and completed tasks on your phone and asks Google Play to show its rating dialog; the counters stay on your phone and Google Play handles the dialog under Google's terms. We add no other analytics.
  • Retention and deletion. Task history, saved tasks, saved details, the downloaded model, your API key, and the browser's cookies, cache and website data (which can keep you signed in to sites) stay on your phone until you delete them. Settings, under Privacy and data, has a button to clear task history and one to delete everything the app stores, including the browser's cookies and cache; the Saved info and Model screens can delete those separately. The step list keeps small screenshots of pages in memory for the current run only; they are never sent anywhere and are gone when you start another task or close the app. The delete-everything button does not clear the rating-library counters described above; uninstalling the app removes everything it stored, including them. Because we hold no copy, there is nothing on our side to delete. The services you connect in Cloud mode, and the websites you visit, keep their own records under their own policies.
